  3. 09Verify that the credential is bound to the holder's deviceKey binding JWT · mdoc DeviceAuth
  4. 10Check the issuer against your trust listETSI TS 119 602 lists of trusted entities

01–04Create the offerClaims check, pre-authorized code, issuer and authorization server metadata
  1. 01Check your claims against the credential type and store them encrypted with the sessionCredential configuration · encrypted at rest
  2. 02Create the offer with a one-time pre-authorized code and an optional PINOpenID4VCI 1.0 · pre-authorized code · tx_code
  3. 03Publish your name, logo and credential types as issuer metadata, signed on requestCredential issuer metadata · signed metadata
  4. 04Act as the wallet's authorization serverOAuth 2.0 authorization server metadata (RFC 8414)
05–08Authorize the walletWallet attestation, single-use code, DPoP-bound token, fresh nonce
  1. 05Check the wallet attestation against your list of trusted wallet providersOAuth 2.0 Attestation-Based Client Authentication · ETSI TS 119 602
  2. 06Redeem the code once and before it expires, and lock it after repeated wrong PINsSingle-use pre-authorized code · tx_code
  3. 07Bind the access token to a key in the walletDPoP (RFC 9449)
  4. 08Hand out a fresh nonce for the key proofOpenID4VCI 1.0 · nonce endpoint
09–15Issue the credentialKey proof, key attestation, status list entry, selective disclosure, signature
  1. 09Verify that the wallet holds the key the credential will be bound toKey proof · c_nonce
  2. 10Check the key attestation that shows the key is kept in secure hardwareKey attestation · key_storage
  3. 11Issue one credential per wallet key, so separate presentations can't be linkedOpenID4VCI 1.0 · batch issuance
  4. 12Reserve a random entry in a status list, so you can revoke or suspend the credential laterToken Status List
  5. 13Build the SD-JWT VC with a salted digest for every claim the holder may withholdSD-JWT VC · selective disclosure
  6. 14Check that your issuer certificate is valid and not revoked, then sign in your HSM or KMSx5c · CRL · PKCS#11 · AWS KMS · Vault

Issuing takes more. One call to POST /api/issuer/offer starts a full OpenID4VCI flow.

With your login in front. In the authorization code flow, EUDIPLO also runs pushed authorization requests, PKCE and the sign-in through Keycloak or any OpenID Connect provider. Claims can also come from your own API at the moment of issuance.

Self-hosted by design

Your servers. Your keys. Your users' data.

EUDIPLO is software you run, not a service you rent. What your users share from their wallets reaches your systems and nobody else's.

A closed identity platform
Walleton the user's phone → Vendor's clouddecrypts every answer, usually holds your keys → Your backend
EUDIPLO
Walleton the user's phone →
Your infrastructure EUDIPLOdecrypts the answer on your server → Your backend

Personal data stays with you

The wallet encrypts its answer to a key your instance creates for that one request. It is decrypted on your server, and deleted or anonymized after a retention period you set, 24 hours by default.

Signing keys in your HSM

Create signing keys in an HSM over PKCS#11, AWS KMS or HashiCorp Vault. They are generated as non-exportable, and every signature happens inside.

Nothing phones home

No analytics, license checks or update checks. Traces and metrics go only to an OpenTelemetry collector you run, or nowhere with OTEL_SDK_DISABLED=true.

For service providers

Offer it as a service, with the same privacy for every customer.

EUDIPLO is multi-tenant: hosting companies, identity providers and public IT service centers can run one installation for many customers. Each tenant has its own keys, certificates, trust lists, configurations, API clients and retention period. espuni runs age verification for online platforms this way.

Why now

The EU has set the dates.

eIDAS 2.0 makes the wallet a standard way to prove who you are online, everywhere in the EU. Services that rely on identity have to be ready to accept it.

  2. 2024

  9. End of 2026

    Every Member State offers at least one EUDI Wallet to its citizens.

  10. End of 2027

Configuration as code

Every credential and verification is a file you can review.

Each configuration is a JSON file with a published schema. Your editor autocompletes it, your CI validates it, and the CLI shows a plan before anything changes.
